# Power Budget Analysis

Watchy is battery-driven and spends the great majority of its life asleep, so every design decision traces back to draining as little as possible. This synthesis ties together the [deep sleep & wake model](../concepts/deep-sleep-wake.md), [RTC_DATA_ATTR persistence](../concepts/rtc-data-attr.md), the [Display](../components/display.md) refresh modes, and the radio-off policy of [WiFi & NTP](../components/wifi-ntp.md) into the watch's energy story. *Verified against the repo at `d1d233c` as of 2026-08.*

**Sleeping is the whole strategy.** The firmware never idles awake: `init()` handles one wake cause and immediately falls into `deepSleep()` (`Watchy.cpp:98`). Most of the time the only wake is the once-per-minute RTC/ timer alarm, so the ESP32 is in deep sleep for ~59.9 of every 60 seconds. Even when an interactive wake (button, accel) occurs, work is bounded — a draw and back to sleep. The official [battery-life documentation](../../sources/2026-08-14-battery-life.md) puts the resulting endurance at **5–7 days with time keeping only**, dropping to **2–3 days when a watch face fetches weather over WiFi**; the docs explicitly attribute the difference to the per-minute wake plus radio-on periods, and suggest extending life by sleeping during off hours, waking only on motion, or lengthening the RTC alarm period when 60-second updates aren't needed.

**Radio discipline.** WiFi and BLE are the largest consumers when active, so they are kept off in sleep and torn down aggressively. Every connection helper ends with `WiFi.mode(WIFI_OFF); btStop()` (e.g., `setupWifi()`, `_getWeatherData()` failure path, `updateFWBegin()`). WiFi is only on for provisioning, NTP sync, and the weather fetch; BLE only for the [BLE OTA](../components/ble-ota.md) update. Connectivity is therefore on-demand, not background.

**Display cost dominates active work.** The 200×200 e-panel's timings are in `Display.h`: full refresh is ~2.6 s, partial refresh ~0.5 s, with ~100 ms power-on and ~150 ms power-off. A per-minute tick uses the **partial** path (`showWatchFace(true)`), which is why the minute update is cheap; a full refresh is reserved for state changes like the reset draw and entering/exiting the menu ([GUI state machine](../concepts/gui-state-machine.md)). `showWatchFace()` also calls `asyncPowerOn()` to overlap panel warmup with drawing, hiding power-on latency behind the actual draw (`Watchy.cpp:665`).

**The accelerometer is always-on yet asleep.** The [BMA423 Accelerometer](../components/bma423-accelerometer.md) runs continuously and, instead of the CPU, uses its own interrupt to wake the ESP32 on step/tilt/double-click. Because the driver object persists in [RTC_DATA_ATTR memory](../concepts/rtc-data-attr.md), its config is written once at reset rather than re-established each wake, avoiding redundant I2C work.

**Leak-age control.** On V1/V2, `deepSleep()` sets all GPIOs to input to stop current leaking out through unconfigured pins, skipping a fixed mask of pins to avoid reset (`Watchy.cpp:119-125`). The [RTC](../components/rtc.md) alarm and the display's hibernate (`display.hibernate()`) ensure even the panel controller draws near-nothing while asleep. V3 additionally configures the I2C bus, USB-detect pin pulls, and 32k RTC clock for a lower-power timer wake.

The net effect: the watch's active draw time is a tiny fraction of each minute, radios are off except on explicit operator action, and the biggest drain inside a wake (full display refresh, WiFi, or BLE) is triggered only when the user actually does something. These choices are what make the difference between a watch that lasts days in active polling and one that lasts months on a wrist.
